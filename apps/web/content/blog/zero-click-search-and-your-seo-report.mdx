Something quietly broke in SEO reporting over the last two years, and most dashboards still render it as a success.

Roughly 60% of Google searches now end without a click on anything. AI Overviews show up in the overwhelming majority of brand-related searches, and where they appear, organic click-through on the results beneath them falls by more than half. The query still happens. Your page still ranks. The visit does not arrive.

If your monthly report leads with impressions and average position, this looks like growth. Impressions climb, because the surface area of search keeps expanding. Position holds, because you did not actually lose the ranking. Traffic flattens or falls, and the report has no vocabulary for why.

## The metric that broke

Average position was always a strange metric — an arithmetic mean over a set of queries with wildly different value — but it used to correlate with something. Position three meant a predictable slice of the clicks. That relationship is what came apart.

Now position three can mean:

- Third result, no AI Overview, roughly the click share you expect.
- Third result underneath an AI Overview that answered the question, with a fraction of that share.
- Third result underneath an AI Overview that cited you, which sends almost no traffic but does shape what the model says about your brand.

Three very different outcomes, one number. Reporting on the average hides the only distinction that matters.

## What to report instead

**Clicks per thousand impressions, per query cluster.** Not sitewide — sitewide CTR averages away the signal. Cluster your queries by intent and track click yield within each cluster. Informational clusters are where zero-click is eating you. Commercial and navigational clusters are usually far more intact. When yield drops inside a cluster while position holds, you are looking at a SERP-feature change, not a ranking problem, and no amount of on-page work will fix it.

**Ranking pages with no click yield at all.** Every site accumulates these: pages that rank respectably and deliver nothing, because the answer they provide is one the SERP now provides itself. They are not failures of the page. They are pages whose job was taken. The useful response is to stop investing in them, not to rewrite them a fourth time.

**Branded search volume as a demand signal.** When people encounter you inside an AI summary or a social feed without clicking, the trace often shows up days later as someone searching your brand name directly. Branded query volume is one of the few places that otherwise-invisible exposure becomes measurable.

## The strategic consequence

If informational content earns fewer clicks per ranking than it did, the return on publishing informational content falls with it. Not to zero — but enough that the calculation changes.

What holds value is content tied to queries where someone is close to a decision, and where the answer is specific enough that a summary cannot substitute for the page. That is exactly the set of queries your paid search account already knows about, because you are paying for them and you can see which ones convert.

This is the argument for connecting the two. A search term with real conversions and no organic page is the highest-confidence content brief you can write, and it is sitting in a report you already run. The list of terms you are renting clicks for is also the list of pages worth building.

## A reporting template that survives contact

Replace the ranking table at the top of your report with three lines:

1. **Click yield by intent cluster**, month over month, with SERP-feature changes annotated.
2. **Revenue-adjacent organic sessions** — sessions to pages that touch a conversion path — rather than total sessions.
3. **Branded query volume**, as a proxy for the exposure you cannot otherwise see.

Rankings still belong in the report. They belong further down, as a diagnostic, not as the headline. The headline should be about whether search is still bringing you people who buy things — which is what everyone reading the report actually wants to know, and what average position stopped answering some time ago.
